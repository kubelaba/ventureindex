Kube

Homelab tinkerer who runs multiple Kubernetes clusters at home — for the fun of figuring out how things actually work.

Who I am

I'm a builder and a tinkerer. I run KubeLaba, a self-hosted homelab where I stand up real infrastructure and break it on purpose until I understand it. Most of what I know, I know because I took it apart.

Something I taught myself

In the last few months I built out GitOps across my home Kubernetes clusters with Flux, so the cluster state lives in Git and reconciles itself instead of me applying manifests by hand. I paired it with Ansible to automate the box-level setup underneath — the parts that used to be a manual slog every time I rebuilt a node.

I learned it the unglamorous way: the official docs, a lot of trial and error, and breaking the cluster repeatedly until the reconcile loop did what I expected. The homelab is the whole point — it's a place where failing is cheap and the only way out is understanding it.

Why I want this

My background is infrastructure and security — networking, cloud, and lately digging into SASE. What pulls me now is the AI layer: how modern AI and AI agents actually work, and what they make buildable that wasn't before. I want to stop wishing for tools and start building them. A studio that backs builders in the AI economy — up the road in Baltimore, no less — is exactly the push I'm looking for.
